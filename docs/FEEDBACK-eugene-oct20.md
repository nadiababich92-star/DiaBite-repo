# Feedback from Eugene Kaganovich, RN, BSN, CDE: 20 October 2026

*He watched the one-minute clip and answered by email. This is a market view from a diabetes educator, not a review of the clinical logic (he did not open the document). Recorded as he wrote it, then what it means for us. The owner decides what changes.*

## What he said

1. **Most US nutrition apps are for tracking** calories and nutrients, mainly for weight. Many insurers already give free apps plus access to registered dietitians for weight and diabetes management.
2. **People with diabetes are busy.** Long hours, family, a budget; they "simply eat what is available and affordable". Some enjoy tracking; **many stop after a few months**.
3. **The effort is the cost:** typing or saying every meal. "What meaningful benefit do they get from doing that?" Calorie apps already show intake, deficiencies, under-eating.
4. **Not a medical tool** (no dosing, no medication), so the value "needs to be very clear and practical".
5. **People eat the same meals again and again,** so tracking becomes repetitive and boring. He himself keeps such apps for about two months, then loses interest.
6. He finds the ideas "creative and interesting" and says he is looking from a real-world, practical side.

## What is true, and what it asks of us

| His point | Where we stand |
|---|---|
| Tracking is abandoned after weeks or months | He is right, and this is the question the beta exists to answer (ROADMAP 3.1: 10 people logging in 4 of 14 days). We have no retention data from outside people yet. Say so; do not argue. |
| Effort must be repaid | The promise has to be a decision, not a diary: "can I eat this?" answered in seconds, before the meal. If the product is experienced as a log, he is right. |
| People repeat meals | The Diary already offers "again" chips (recent meals in one tap) and the Menu can plan a week. Whether that is enough is a session question. |
| Busy, budget, "what is available" | Not addressed: the product knows nothing of price or what is in the fridge. A real gap, and a roadmap question, not a promise. |
| Insurers already give free apps and dietitians | The nearest competitor is not another app but a benefit people already have. This also suggests a channel: be useful to the dietitians, not instead of them. |
| Not a medical tool | Deliberate (no doses, no diagnosis). It means the value cannot be "managing diabetes"; it has to be the plain daily one. |

## Ideas, not decisions

- Test the claim directly in the five sessions: how many seconds from opening the app to a verdict; would they open it before a meal tomorrow; what would make them stop. Ask what they use now and why they stopped.
- Measure repeat use honestly in the beta and publish the number, whichever way it goes.
- Think about "what is in my kitchen" and "what does it cost" as the first answer to "busy and on a budget".
- Treat dietitians and programs (DPP, DSMES, insurer programs) as the channel to test next.
