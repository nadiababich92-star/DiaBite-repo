# Spec: the shopping list that follows the plan

*From `docs/engineering/engineering-doc.md`, sections 3 and 8. Read `plan-data-and-rules.md` first.*

## Files

`src/lib/menu.ts` (new pure function), `src/components/MenuPage.tsx` (the source switch and the heading), `src/lib/storage.ts` (the ticked-lines key, see below), `docs/design.md` (nothing new).

## `shoppingFromEntries` (pure, `src/lib/menu.ts`)

```ts
export type ShoppingLine = { name: string; grams: number; category: FoodCategory }
export function shoppingFromEntries(entries: DiaryEntry[], today: string, recipes: (id: string) => Dish | undefined): ShoppingLine[]
```

1. **Which entries:** `isPlanned(e)` and `e.date >= today` and `e.date <= addDays(today, MAX_AHEAD_DAYS)`. Eaten entries, and planned entries on a past day, are never shopping.
2. **What an entry contributes:**
   - an entry whose `foodId` starts with `rec:` — the recipe from `recipes(id)`; every ingredient `{name, grams, category}` at `grams * scale`, where `scale = entry.grams / dish.servingGrams` when the dish has a serving weight, else `entry.snapshot?.servings ?? 1`. A recipe that cannot be found contributes its snapshot name and `entry.grams` under category `other` (it is still shopping, and it is not invented);
   - a curated dish entry (its `foodId` names a menu dish, or the entry came from the Menu): the dish's `items` (`getFood(item.foodId)`) and `ingredients` at `m.scale` as `shoppingList` does today — the Menu stores the scale in `grams / dish.servingGrams`;
   - any other entry (a plain food or an answer-logged food): the food's name and `entry.grams`, category from `getFood(entry.foodId)` when it exists in the local table, else `other`.
3. **Aggregation** exactly as `shoppingList`: one line per food, key `name.trim().toLowerCase()`, grams summed over **all** days; under 5 g across the whole span is dropped; to the nearest 5 g; sorted by category then name. Refactor the shared tail (the `filter/map/sort`) into one helper used by both functions, so the two cannot drift apart.
4. The function reads no clock and no storage; `today` is passed.

## The Menu screen

- A heading line above the list names its source: **Shopping list for what you planned** (planned entries exist) or **Shopping list for the week** (none do; the generated week's `shoppingList(plan)` as today).
- When the source is the plan, a quiet line under the heading says the span: **From Oct 16 to Oct 22.** (the first and last planned date in the list, `en-US` short month and day).
- A person with a plan and a generated week sees the plan's list; a small link **Use the generated week instead** switches the source for this view (not remembered) and, on the week's list, **Use what I planned** switches back. The link appears only when both sources have lines.
- The copy-to-clipboard text (`shoppingText`) works for either source unchanged.
- **Ticked lines:** `diabite.shopping.v1` keeps ticks only while the list they belong to is the one on screen. For the plan source the key is the sorted entry ids joined by `,`; for the week source it is as now. A changed plan starts a fresh list (nothing is carried over: a wrong tick that hides something to buy is worse than a lost tick).
- Empty plan and empty week: the existing empty state.

## What it does not do

No quantities are generated by a model; the sums are the grams already stored or already in the recipe. It does not subtract what is in the person's kitchen, and it does not know a day was eaten unless the entry was marked eaten (then it drops out of the list).

## Verifiable

`plan-evals.md` cases 5–6: planned only, from today forward; an eaten entry and a past planned entry excluded; a recipe expanded and scaled; the 5 g rule across days; the same food on two days summed; no planned entries gives `[]` from `shoppingFromEntries` and the Menu falls back to `shoppingList(plan)` byte for byte (compare the two outputs).
Screen: the Menu with a plan and with none, at 390 px, light and dark.
